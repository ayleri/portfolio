# ASCII ocean

Adapted from [stackedlol/ascii-sharks](https://github.com/stackedlol/ascii-sharks), commit a0074c16a106f0abc665fe9712ca445f8f98ad8e (MIT; copyright 2026 drewdi). This is the simulation credited by [ASCII Ocean on Framer](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/ascii-ocean/). The original license is included in `LICENSE` and published at `/licenses/ascii-ocean.txt`.

`species.ts` and `math.ts` contain the upstream simulation helpers. `world.ts` removes the headline avoidance area, scales the scene with the viewport, and allows sharks to swim beyond the frame with gentle steering back into the scene. `renderer.ts` controls ASCII resolution directly with 36–160 columns and a target cell width of 11px, and draws the grid and rose dash fish on white, with a muted palette assigned by species. Shark size is independent of grid resolution. `engine.ts` provides the animation lifecycle, pause fades, passive hover repulsion and click splashes, and static reduced-motion rendering. Animals keep moving during the pause fade, then the loop stops after 900ms. `AsciiOcean.astro` provides the canvas/control markup and scoped styles, including the eased opacity mask. Its external script, `js/ascii-ocean.mjs`, registers the custom element and wires up controls, motion preferences, and lifecycle cleanup.

The initial scene in `engine.ts` includes five sharks: a dusty rose classic (#A65D73), a deep plum hammerhead (#564147), a muted mauve tiger (#BEA5AB), a white thresher with soft gray lettering, and a gray nurse shark. All eight species remain supported by the renderer. Species colors live in `renderer.ts`; the center-column mask and pause fades apply to all of them.

`shark-separation.ts` handles look-ahead avoidance and whole-body separation, including fins and tail motion. Spawning separates only the arrival. Default sizes span 0.55–1.6, with the gray nurse at 0.85; smaller sharks cruise faster, while the giant default hammerhead retains its explicit speed multiplier of 0.4. Overall simulation speed is 0.8, retaining the relative speed differences.
